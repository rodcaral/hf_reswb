## 3. Observation transformations used

Faithful to `F-033`'s own original method, restated precisely:

1. For each Series, group observations by calendar date (`date(observed_at)`); where a CEDEAR
   Series carries multiple intraday rows for one date, take the **last** (latest `observed_at`)
   as that date's representative value — matching `ImpliedFxDiagnosticService._latest_leg_
   observation()`'s own established convention, not a new rule invented for this retest.
2. **Daily implied FX** for a pair on a date = `CEDEAR_value(date) / underlying_value(date)` —
   the raw ratio, without applying any curated `POINT`/`PERIOD` ratio-applicability assertion or
   the legacy `Series.ratio` scalar. This intentionally isolates the *price data itself* from
   any ratio-evidence question — `F-033` concerns whether the underlying price streams are
   independent, not whether a ratio fact is correctly curated (a separate, already-closed
   question, §15f–§15k).
3. **Day-over-day return** for a date = `(implied_fx(date) − implied_fx(prev_date)) /
   implied_fx(prev_date)`, where `prev_date` is the immediately preceding date **present in that
   pair's own joined series** — no calendar-day gap-filling, no interpolation.
4. **Missing-date/alignment treatment**: a date enters a pair's own daily-implied-FX series only
   if **both** legs have at least one observation on that date (inner join on date). Pairwise
   correlation between two members is computed only over dates present in **both** members' own
   return series (a second inner join) — the sample size `n` for each pairwise comparison is
   reported explicitly, never assumed equal across pairs.

## 6. Direct value-level check — beyond correlation, a stronger test

Correlation alone can be produced by two genuinely distinct-but-perfectly-scaled series. This
retest went further: computed the **raw ratio between MSFT's and MELI's own daily implied-FX
values** (`implied_fx_MSFT(date) / implied_fx_MELI(date)`), independently of any correlation
statistic.

**Result: this ratio was `1.0000000000` — exactly, to 10 decimal places — on every one of the
54 consecutive trading dates from `2026-05-29` through `2026-08-17` inclusive.** Two genuinely
independent companies (Microsoft, a US technology company; MercadoLibre, a Latin American
e-commerce company) with entirely different real share prices, entirely different real CEDEAR
conversion ratios (`30:1` vs `120:1`), cannot produce a bit-for-bit identical price *ratio*
across their own CEDEAR/underlying legs on 54 separate trading days by any market mechanism —
this is definitive, not merely suggestive.

**A precisely dated transition was found and is reported as new evidence, not previously named
in this repository's own prior `F-033` records**: on `2026-08-18`, the MSFT/MELI implied-FX
ratio abruptly shifted from exactly `1.0000000000` to approximately `4.00` (observed range
`3.9800857738`–`4.0127467732` across `2026-08-18`–`2026-09-02`, still visibly non-market-noisy —
a tight band around a new constant, not a resumption of independent variation). **`2026-08-18`
is the exact date this repository's own `7f7f73c` commit populated GATE 1's CEDEAR relationships
and produced the primary five-pair dispersion/CV calibration evidence later downgraded at
§15d** — the transition date coincides exactly with that data-population activity, though this
retest does not determine causation, only the coincidence of dates.

QQQ's own MSFT-relative ratio shows the same structural pattern — a materially noisier but still
narrow band before `2026-08-18` (`0.92`–`0.99`, day-to-day movement present but bounded far
tighter than genuine market noise would produce against two unrelated instruments) and a
different, tighter band after (`0.665`–`0.667`) — consistent with QQQ being tied to the same
underlying mechanism by a different, less exact coupling than MSFT/MELI's own near-perfect one.

## 7. Whether any observation stream, import job, or derived data is mechanically reused

Checked directly: `observation.import_run_id`/`origin_import_run_id` for all three flagged
pairs' both legs, around the `2026-08-18` transition date. **No literal row-level or import-run-
level reuse found** — each Series (all six legs across the three flagged pairs) has its own
distinct `import_run_id` for its own `2026-08-18`-window rows (e.g. CEDEAR `import_run_id`s
`81063`/`81065`/`81067` — sequential, not identical, consistent with a per-instrument fetch loop
run in the same batch, not literal duplication of one row across series). **This rules out the
simplest possible explanation** (a storage-layer bug writing one series' rows into another's) —
the values stored for MSFT and MELI are genuinely separate rows, separately fetched/imported,
that nonetheless track each other with a fixed or near-fixed multiplier. This is consistent with
`F-033`'s own original diagnosis: **either (a) both series' underlying source values are
mathematically derived from one shared input with a per-series multiplier upstream of storage,
or (b) an acquisition-side construction/transformation applies the same computed factor to
multiple nominally-distinct tickers** — this retest's own evidence cannot distinguish (a) from
(b), matching the original filing's own stated limitation, and this retest does not attempt to.
